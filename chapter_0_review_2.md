## Factual issues worth fixing

**1. §0.2 — energy per cavity mode is wrong, and inconsistent with your own formula.** You write the Rayleigh–Jeans derivation as "applying the equipartition theorem `⟨E⟩ = ½ k_B T` per mode," then two clauses later give the law as `u(ν) ∝ ν² k_B T` (full `k_B T`). Equipartition gives ½`k_B T` *per quadratic degree of freedom*; a cavity mode is dynamically a harmonic oscillator with two such terms, so it's `k_B T` *per mode*. Either say "½`k_B T` per degree of freedom (hence `k_B T` per mode, since each has two)" or just "`k_B T` per mode." As written it's both incorrect and internally inconsistent — a sharp reader will catch it, and this chapter sets a high enough bar that the slip is conspicuous.

**2. §0.6 — the 10⁻⁷ significance claim is false for one of the three 2015 tests.** "All three reported `|S|` significantly above 2, with statistical significance … below `10⁻⁷`" holds for the *photonic* experiments (Giustina, Shalm), but the Delft NV-centre result (Hensen et al.) reported a p-value around 0.039 — only marginal — because the entanglement-swap event rate was very low. That weakness was the price of being the first loophole-free test on a matter-based platform over 1.3 km, and a 2016 Delft run improved the statistics. Recommend splitting the claim: attribute the very high significance to the photonic platforms, and note Delft was historically first / first on matter but statistically weaker.

**3. §0.12 — Simon was not Shor's "office neighbour at Bell Labs."** Shor was at AT&T Bell Labs (New Providence); Simon was not there. The documented route is better than the one you have: Simon submitted to STOC 1993 and was rejected, but Shor was on the program committee, immediately saw the potential for applying the structure to factoring and discrete logarithms, tried unsuccessfully to get Simon's paper accepted, then developed his own — and both eventually appeared together. The real anecdote (Shor encountered it as a *reviewer of the rejected paper*) is more interesting than office proximity and is on the record in Simon's own words. Also minor: Simon's work is more accurately 1993 (the rejected submission) even though the published version is 1994.

**4. §0.12 — NIST status is stale.** You have "ML-KEM (FIPS 203) and ML-DSA (FIPS 204), finalised in August 2024, with SLH-DSA (FIPS 205) and FIPS 206 / FIPS 207 in progress." But FIPS 203, 204, and 205 were all finalised together, effective August 14, 2024 — so SLH-DSA is *done*, not in progress. FALCON was selected and will be published as FIPS 206 (in development); HQC was selected for standardization on March 11, 2025 as an additional KEM. There is no "FIPS 207" in the track. Corrected: "ML-KEM (FIPS 203), ML-DSA (FIPS 204), and SLH-DSA (FIPS 205), all finalised August 2024, with FN-DSA (FIPS 206, FALCON-based) in draft and HQC selected March 2025 as a fourth-round KEM for future standardization." This is a `current/stale` flip exactly of the type your linter is meant to catch.

**Two smaller ones, optional:**

- §0.4: the rigorous `Δx·Δp ≥ ℏ/2` bound is Kennard (1927) and Weyl, not Heisenberg's own 1927 argument, which was heuristic (`~ℏ`). Given how cleanly you handled the parallel Dirac-1939 caveat, a one-clause "(rigorously, Kennard 1927)" would be consistent with the chapter's own standard.
- §0.11: "a quantum-mechanical Hamiltonian whose *ground-state evolution* simulates the time evolution of a Turing machine" — Benioff's model is the unitary *time* evolution under the Hamiltonian simulating a (reversible) TM, not ground-state evolution. Drop "ground-state."

## Structure and pedagogy

The thing I'd most push on is the **front-matter stack**. Before §0.1 you have three framing blocks — the opening prose, the "How to read this chapter" callout, and the "thesis in one paragraph" — and all three state the same central arc. It's a lot of throat-clearing, and the repetition slightly dilutes each statement. I'd merge to one framing block and let §0.1 arrive sooner.

Second, the **`**Name (year).**` lead format** is used in nearly every entry across all 16 sections. It's great for scannability, but it has two costs: it gets monotonous over a chapter this long, and it visually foregrounds exactly the names-and-dates the prose explicitly tells the reader *not* to memorise. The format fights the instruction. I'd vary the rhythm — let some passages (§0.7, the early parts of §0.6) run as narrative prose rather than annotated entries.

Third, **§0.14 is the least prelude-like section.** It's a compressed Chapter 20, and it's long and list-heavy. Since you explicitly defer the full platform treatment to Ch. 20–22, the prelude version can afford to thin the per-platform detail and keep the historical throughline: Cirac–Zoller as the first engineering blueprint → NMR as proving ground → DiVincenzo as the convergent checklist → diversification → no winner yet. Density is itself a quality concern for a chapter billed as a prelude, even with the page count relaxed.

Finally, two light touches: several of the italicised bridging sentences (the Benioff/Feynman/Deutsch trio especially) restate the paragraph they follow rather than bridging to the next — tightening them would sharpen the causal-arc feel you're going for. And the "not magic parallelism / information is physical" theses recur across §0.7, §0.11, §0.12, and §0.16; the §0.16 recap is explicitly a recap, so you can trust it to carry those and trim the mid-chapter restatements.

None of the structural points are blocking — this is already a publishable-quality chapter. The four factual corrections are the ones I'd treat as must-fix before it goes further.
