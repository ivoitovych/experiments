- **Entertainment:** 1/5 (actively dry or frustrating), 2/5 (functional but flat), 3/5 (solid), 4/5 (engaging), 5/5 (exceptionally compelling).
- “No defect found” means the unit was inspected; it does not claim mathematical proof or external experimental verification.
- Archived material is evaluated for archival usefulness, labeling, provenance, and risk of confusing maintainers—not as if it were current book copy.

#### §0.1 opening and five classical assumptions (lines 29–43)

- **Pedagogy:** Naming the assumptions before breaking them is excellent advance organization. It gives readers a
  retrieval frame for a name-heavy chapter.
- **Major historical/conceptual suspicion:** “Separability—the joint state ... is fully specified by the states
  of its parts” is not a generic classical assumption without qualification. Classical correlated joint
  probability distributions are not fixed by their marginals either. What classical theories typically allow is
  a complete joint state assembled from definite subsystem variables plus correlations; entanglement defeats
  separability in a more specific sense. Current wording risks teaching an overstrong classical/quantum contrast.
- **Precision suspicion:** “Commutativity—the order in which two measurements ... does not affect the results”
  is also too universal as classical practice: invasive classical measurements can disturb systems and order can

  of approximation should be distinguished.
- **Grammar:** “and any time we discretise” is not parallel with the preceding “in ... , in ...”; use “and whenever”

- **Physical subtlety:** A circuit-level unitary need not be generated as one uninterrupted evolution under a single
  fixed implicit Hamiltonian. It may be a controlled, time-dependent pulse sequence or compiled composite. Any
  finite-dimensional unitary has a Hermitian logarithm, but that mathematical generator need not be the hardware’s
  realized Hamiltonian or be unique.

#### §5.4 three interpretations and POVM extension

- **Irreversibility overstatement:** Conditional projective state update is nonunitary and generally cannot be
  inverted from the post-measurement system state alone. Yet a measurement can be modeled by reversible unitary
  coupling to an apparatus/environment before information is discarded, and special nondisturbing cases preserve the
  input. “You cannot recover” needs “in general, from the system and outcome alone.”
- **Repeated-measurement ambiguity:** “Repeated measurements of the same observable on the same state can yield
  different outcomes” is true across freshly prepared identical states. Immediate repetition on the same physical
  system after an ideal projective measurement yields the same outcome. This sentence could actively seed a
  misconception unless “freshly prepared copies” replaces “the same state.”
- **Basis language:** General projective measurements are decompositions into subspaces, not necessarily a basis of
  one-dimensional vectors. “Measurement basis” is fine for the common nondegenerate circuit case but conflicts
  slightly with the section’s more general opening.
- **POVM definition:** Positivity plus $\sum_mE_m=I$ already implies $E_m\preceq I$, so explicitly stating both bounds
  is redundant but pedagogically harmless.
- **POVM state-update gap:** Effects $E_m$ determine probabilities, not post-measurement states. Since the projective
  discussion prominently includes collapse, the POVM paragraph should warn that Kraus operators/instruments are
  required to specify disturbance and conditional states.
- **Scope nuance:** Adaptive measurement strategies are sequences/decision policies built from instruments; a POVM
  can represent their net classical outcome statistics, but saying POVMs themselves “capture ... adaptive
  strategies” compresses important temporal structure.
- **Entertainment: 4/5.** The three-item interpretation block is energetic and memorable, though the repeated-
  measurement line creates drama by sacrificing precision.

#### §5.5 composite-system postulate

- **Correctness:** Tensor-product composition, product-state construction, subsystem operator lifting, and local PVM
  lifting are correct in the finite-dimensional distinguishable-subsystem setting used by circuit computing.
- **Notation collision:** Naming an operator on subsystem $A$ itself `$A$` collides with the system label. A symbol
  such as $M_A$ would make `$M_A\otimes I_B$` immediately parseable.
- **Identical-particle caveat:** The bare tensor-product postulate needs qualification outside distinguishable
  subsystems because bosonic/fermionic state spaces obey symmetry restrictions. The chapter explicitly limits itself
  to gate-model finite dimensions, so one sentence is enough rather than a detour.
- **Resource claim:** The exponential ambient dimension is necessary for the usual qubit-state representation, but
  calling it a necessary condition for quantum computational advantage is too strong as a general theorem. Advantage
  claims depend on encoding and resource model, and restricted submanifolds/subspaces can still support useful
  processing.
- **Entanglement claim:** Entanglement does distinguish nonseparable quantum states from classical joint probability
  models in an important sense, but “the resource behind ... most quantum-algorithm speedups” is contested and too
  sweeping. Some mixed-state models show nonclassical advantage with little or no standard entanglement, while large
  entanglement alone is insufficient. State the safer claim that it is central to the named protocols and many
  algorithms.
- **Local measurement wording:** `$P_m\otimes I_B$` leaves $B$ dynamically untouched only at the operator-action
  level. Conditional on an outcome, an entangled $B$ subsystem’s reduced state can change (steering), while its
  unconditioned reduced state does not. This distinction is important enough to flag here.
- **Source-format defect:** Doubled braces recur in the projector set.
- **Entertainment: 4/5.** The section conveys the scale jump cleanly and avoids the usual “$2^n$ amplitudes therefore

#### §5.6 closing equivalence claim

#### §5.7 amplitudes versus classical probabilities

- **Correctness:** The qubit probabilities, phase-bearing amplitudes, orthogonality of $|+\rangle$ and $|-\rangle$,

- **Postulate reference:** The result uses the state and measurement postulates, currently numbered 1 and 3; this is
  internally consistent with the section labels but inconsistent with the introduction’s “Postulate 4” reference.
- **Classical-model overstatement:** A classical probabilistic computer is not “fully described by those two weights”
  between preparation and output; it can have hidden/internal state, transition kernels, correlations, and a
  computation history. For a fixed binary output distribution those weights characterize only the final marginal.
  The real contrast is with coherent transformations that depend on relative phase.
- **Fundamentality claim:** Phase is vital, but “the” fundamental reason for quantum/classical computational

  restrictions, and the allowed operation set all enter different explanations of advantage.
- **Global-phase caveat:** “The additional structure is phase” should say **relative** phase. A state vector also
  carries relationships across basis amplitudes, while global phase is physically redundant, as the next section

- **Terminology:** “the operations that move it are unitaries” is evocative but imprecise: unitaries can convert
  relative phase into amplitude differences in a chosen basis; channels and measurements can also alter or reveal
  phase information.
- **Entertainment: 5/5.** The $|+\rangle/|-\rangle$ reveal is compact, concrete, and one of the chapter’s best

#### §5.8 global versus relative phase

- **Correctness:** Ray equivalence, invariance of all measurement statistics under a global phase, and the
  operational role of relative phase are explained correctly.
- **Domain edge case:** The ratio $\beta/\alpha$ is undefined when $\alpha=0$. The overlap-based “equivalent”
  description has the same reference-component problem. Define relative phase only when both amplitudes are nonzero,
  or discuss pairwise phase differences among nonzero components.
- **Unitary quantifier:** A changed relative phase does not interfere differently under **any** subsequent unitary
  mixing the components. Some mixing operations and measurements can remain insensitive for particular states/phases.
  Use “can interfere differently under suitable subsequent unitaries.”
- **Born-rule defect:** “The Born rule throws away global phase but preserves relative phase” is misleading. A fixed
  basis Born measurement also discards relative phase from its output probabilities. Quantum evolution plus a
  different measurement can make relative phase observable; the Born rule itself maps amplitudes to probabilities.
- **Conceptual overstatement:** Calling this “the most important” difference from a probability simplex is subjective
  and hides convex-versus-projective geometry, noncommuting measurements, and composition. “A central difference” is
  enough.
- **Algorithm caveat:** Global phase remains irrelevant when it is truly common to the entire closed state. A phase
  that is “global” only on one branch of a controlled operation becomes relative and observable. This is a valuable
  warning for controlled-$U$ constructions.
- **Cross-reference suspicion:** Phase gates are said to be defined in Chapter 6 despite the earlier gate taxonomy
  pointing to Chapter 8. Verify numbering.
- **Entertainment: 5/5.** The debugging corollary is reassuring and immediately useful; adding the controlled-branch
  trap would make the section even more memorable.

#### §5.9 definitions and computing motivation

- **Definition:** “Not a rank-one projector” is correct for mixed density operators, assuming normalized

- **Pure-state special-case defect:** A pure density operator can have infinitely many trivial ensemble

#### §5.10 density-operator definition, ensembles, and purity

- **Correctness:** Hermiticity, positivity, unit trace, convex decompositions into normalized rank-one projectors,

#### §5.13 no-cloning theorem statement and proof

- **Theorem scope:** The standard impossibility allows the cloning machine an ancillary/environment state and asks
  for identical output copies, possibly with a final machine state. Stating only a unitary on
  `$\mathcal H\otimes\mathcal H$` with no ancillary output proves a narrower model. It still rules out that simple
  cloner, but the advertised universal theorem should either include ancillas or invoke preservation of fidelity for
  general channels.
- **State normalization:** The proof assumes normalized input and blank states; Postulate 1 supplies this for states,
  but stating it locally would make the inner-product cancellation explicit.
- **“Distinct” is insufficient:** Two distinct state vectors can differ only by global phase and represent the same
  physical state; even distinct rays can be orthogonal. The contradiction requires a pair of nonorthogonal,
  nonidentical rays, which the proof later correctly selects.
- **Complex-overlap algebra:** Solving `$z=z^2$` indeed gives $z\in\{0,1\}$ even for complex $z$. However an overlap of
  magnitude one may be a phase for equivalent rays; the exact equality to 1 partly reflects phase conventions imposed
  by the desired vector-output equation. Framing in rays or absolute fidelity avoids this representational wrinkle.
- **Linearity alternative:** A two-line superposition proof could complement this inner-product proof, but the current
  proof is cleaner because it directly identifies preservation of distinguishability.
- **Source-format defect:** Doubled spacing and set-brace commands recur.
- **Citation need:** Named historical attribution and the precise year should be backed by bibliography entries or
  citations, not only parenthetical names.
- **Entertainment: 5/5.** The overlap squaring contradiction is elegant, fast, and inherently satisfying.

#### §5.13 consequences, exceptions, and approximate cloning

- **QKD overclaim:** “Quantum key distribution is secure” does not follow from no-cloning alone. Security also depends
  on disturbance/information tradeoffs, protocol proofs, authentication, implementation assumptions, finite-key
  effects, and side-channel control. No-cloning is motivating intuition, not a sufficient security proof.
- **Known-state wording:** Copying a “known” state is possible by independently preparing it; a device given a single
  physical specimen without a classical description cannot necessarily clone it just because someone knows the
  label. Distinguish state preparation from copying the supplied system.
- **Orthogonal-set nuance:** A unitary can perfectly clone any mutually orthogonal set even when the particular input
  label is unknown; the final paragraph’s classical computational-basis example conveys this but the rule should be
  stated explicitly.
- **Error-correction precision:** Quantum codes do distribute logical information redundantly without producing
  independent copies. “Entangled multi-qubit states” is broadly right, though some encoded basis codewords may be
  product states; it is the encoded subspace and syndrome extraction/no-cloning-compatible redundancy that matter.
- **Approximate-cloner qualification:** The $5/6$ figure is for optimal symmetric universal $1\!\to\!2$ qubit cloning
  under the usual single-copy fidelity. State-dependent, asymmetric, probabilistic, and phase-covariant cloners have
  different bounds. Include “symmetric” and define fidelity at least briefly.
- **Likely factual defect:** “the bound sharpens as you ask for more copies” is ambiguous and possibly backwards in
  wording. For universal $1\to M$ qubit cloning, individual-clone fidelity decreases toward the state-estimation
  limit as $M$ grows; say this directly.
- **Cross-reference risk:** Verify Chapters 19 and 27, including whether Chapter 27 actually develops optimal-cloning
  attack analysis rather than only mentioning it.
- **Entertainment: 5/5.** The practical consequences are compelling, but the QKD certainty is exciting at the cost of
  accuracy.

#### §5.14 conservation interpretation and consequences

- **Major conceptual overstatement:** “Quantum information is conserved in a way classical information is not” and
  the `rm -f` contrast are misleading. Closed reversible classical dynamics also conserves information; physical
  erasure moves entropy/information to an environment and has a thermodynamic cost (Landauer). Classical copying is
  allowed because classical distinguishable states can be cloned by reversible gates, but classical erasure is not
